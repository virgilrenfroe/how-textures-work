Where you see this

Real people in real jobs use these maps every day. Here is where the ideas from this lesson show up.

  • Games

    Texture artist

    A texture artist paints the color, roughness, and normal maps that wrap onto a character or prop through its UVs. A normal map lets a simple model show stitches, scratches, and rivets without adding extra shape that would slow the game down.

  • Film and animation

    Look-development artist

    A look-development artist decides how every surface in a shot reacts to light, and uses roughness maps to set where a costume or prop is shiny and where it is dull. For close-ups they often use displacement, so the bumps really change the shape and cast true shadows.

  • Online stores

    3D product visualization artist

    A 3D product visualization artist makes product pictures from 3D models instead of photos. Keeping color and roughness as separate maps lets a matte label, a glossy cap, and brushed metal each catch light the way the real product does.

  • Car paint and fabric

    Automotive visualization artist

    An automotive visualization artist builds digital car paint, leather, and seat fabric for car configurators and advertisements. Fine normal or bump maps carry the weave of a fabric and the grain of leather, and roughness controls how sharp the reflections in the paint look.

  • Architecture

    Architectural visualization artist

    An architectural visualization artist shows buildings before they are built. Height and normal maps give brick, stone, and wood real-looking depth, and weathering masks add rust stains and water marks so a design does not look fake-new.

For teachers

Short objectives, one classroom loop, and how the lab maps to studio practice and materials science.

Learning objectives

  • Explain what UV coordinates do on a simple solid.
  • Separate albedo (color) from roughness (shine variation).
  • Contrast normal maps (fake bumps) with displacement (real geometry).
  • Describe weathering as a mask that drives several material channels.

10-minute classroom activity

1) Bring a cereal box. 2) Students sketch the six faces as a cross (UV unwrap) and draw a logo on one face. 3) Open Specimen 01 and match where the logo would land on a box. 4) Finish on Specimen 03: write one sentence on why a normal-map brick wall would cast a flat shadow.
